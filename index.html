Why CCPS works for you

Guaranteed equity, at the cheaper of a ₹20 Cr cap or a 20% discount. Your price does not rise if the company does.

Holding Period (Current – Sept 2028)

You own the conversion right

Your money goes in as Compulsorily Convertible Preference Shares. Equity is guaranteed: you convert at whichever price gives you more shares — the ₹20 Cr cap, or 20% below the next round. If the company is worth far more later, your entry price does not move up with it.

Trigger Milestone: Sept 30, 2028

You convert into equity

On 30 Sept 2028, or sooner if a qualified round closes, your CCPS becomes equity. You take the lower price of the two below, which is the one that increases your stake:

Scenario A: Next Round is > ₹25 Cr

You convert at the ₹20 Cr cap. A later round can be many times higher; your price stays at ₹20 Cr, so the same cheque buys a larger share of that value.

Scenario B: Next Round is < ₹25 Cr

You still convert at a 20% discount to that round. New investors pay full price; you pay less per share.

Why OCD works for you

18% a year while you wait, then your choice: discounted equity, or cash repaid to you with interest still running.

Months 0 – 24

Your principal earns 18% while you decide

You hold an Optionally Convertible Debenture. For 24 months your principal accrues 18% a year. You are not forced into equity. At the end of that period you pick the path that pays you more.

Month 24 Milestone

You choose the better outcome

At month 24 you elect one path. Both are yours to take:

🔄 Option A: Conversion

Principal plus 24 months of interest converts into equity at a 15% discount. You receive shares for more than the cash you put in, at a lower price than the round.

💸 Option B: Repayment Exit

You take cash instead. Principal plus 24 months of interest is repaid to you over the next 24 months, with 18% still running on the balance.

Months 25 – 48 (If Repayment Chosen)

You are paid out in 24 instalments

If you choose repayment, you receive 24 monthly instalments. The 18% rate keeps accruing on the unpaid balance, so the total cash you collect is higher than principal plus the first two years of interest.
